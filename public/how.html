How Greenlit decides

Every rule the app applies, the numbers behind it, and where each one comes from. Last updated: 24 November 2026

The short version. Greenlit reads what is printed on a product’s label and compares it with the needs you entered and with what public health bodies and regulators have published. It describes ingredients and numbers. It does not test products, it does not judge companies, and it is not paid by anyone. Every result is worked out on your phone by fixed rules: the same product and the same profile always give the same answer. No AI decides a result.

What the words mean

Nothing flagged / Fits your profileNothing in the listed ingredients conflicts with what you entered.
No allergens foundYou entered an allergy. None of your allergens is in the ingredient list or the allergen tags. The app does not say “safe”: the data may be out of date, so read the pack.
Not ideal for youNothing you must avoid, but it works against a health need or an avoid choice you entered. The reason is shown with the number.
Not for you / Not suitableThe label lists something your profile rules out: an allergen, an ingredient your diet excludes, or something not for that person’s age.
Not for this agePublished guidance says not to give it at that age.
Check the labelThe data is missing, garbled, too old, or in a language the app cannot check. The app will not guess.

These words describe how a product fits your profile. They are not a statement about the product’s quality or its maker.

Allergies

  • An allergen named in the ingredient list, or in the database’s allergen tags, gives “Not for you”. A “may contain” line counts.
  • A clean result is given only when the list is in English, French, German, Spanish or Italian, at least 40% of it is recognised by the database, and the entry was refreshed within five years. Otherwise: “Check the label”.
  • Every clean result shows when the entry was last refreshed, and a button to check your own pack from a photo.

Bands: 80–100 “Fits every day”, 60–79 “Fits most days”, 40–59 “Fits sometimes”, 20–39 “Fits occasionally”, 0–19 “Fits rarely”. Every point added or removed is listed under the score. The thresholds are Greenlit’s own choices; the Nutri-Score grade is a public standard.

Cosmetics, supplements, pet food and infant formula are not scored: no official score exists for them, so the app does not invent one.

The app cannot see texture, so apart from plain whole nuts it cannot warn about choking. Infant formula is never scored, labelled or compared: talk to your paediatrician.

Supplements

  • A day’s servings of each vitamin and mineral are compared with the Tolerable Upper Intake Level, the highest daily amount likely to pose no risk. Source: Health Canada, Dietary Reference Intakes (shared with the US National Academies).
  • Caffeine is compared with the 400 mg a day the US FDA cites for most adults.
  • St. John’s wort carries the medicines warning from the US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health.
  • The app does not say whether a supplement works, cannot detect contamination, and does not check for children under 4.

Cosmetics

The app lists ingredients that a regulator has banned or restricted, naming the rule each time, mainly the EU Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009 and its updates such as Regulation 2021/1902. A rule in one region is stated as a rule in that region, not as a worldwide fact.

Pets

Foods are checked for ingredients the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center lists as harmful to dogs or cats. A human food with none of them is never shown as suitable for a pet, only as “no toxins found”.

How processed is it?

The group shown (1 to 4) is the NOVA classification, developed by researchers at the University of São Paulo and assigned to each product by Open Food Facts. It describes how a food is made. When the database has no group, the app may show its own estimate, clearly marked as an estimate.

Others on this shelf

Shown only when you ask. A product is listed only if it is of the same kind, sold in your country, scanned by enough people to be a real and checked entry, passes your whole profile, contains no sweeteners or added caffeine, and is not higher in sugar, salt or saturated fat. The line under each is a fact from its label. Nobody can pay to appear, and Greenlit earns nothing from any product.

Where the data comes from, and its limits

  • Open Food Facts, Open Beauty Facts and Open Pet Food Facts: volunteer-run public databases.
  • USDA FoodData Central, the NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database and openFDA recall notices: US government data.
  • Entries can be wrong, incomplete or out of date, and makers change recipes. The printed pack is always the authority. If an entry is wrong, each result links to where it can be corrected for everyone.

What Greenlit does not do

  • It does not test or inspect products.
  • It does not give medical, dietary or veterinary advice.
  • It does not rate companies or brands.
  • It takes no money from manufacturers, shows no ads and sells nothing.
