Privacy & Terms

Last updated: 23 November 2026

The short version. Greenlit has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no server of its own. Your profile and history stay on your phone. The app talks to two outside services, and only when you ask it to: Open Food Facts (to look up a barcode) and Google (only if you choose to use the optional AI extras with your own key).

What stays on your device

  • Your profile — the allergies, dietary rules, health watch items and goal you select. This is health-related information. It is stored only in this app's storage on your device and is never sent to us or anyone else.
  • Your scan history — the last 20 products you checked, and two counters shown on the home screen.
  • Your Google Gemini API key, if you add one.
  • A few settings, such as whether you have seen the welcome screen.

We cannot see any of this. There is no Greenlit account and no Greenlit server holding user data.

What leaves your device, and when

  • Barcode lookups. When you scan or type a barcode, the app requests that product from Open Food Facts, a public food database. Open Food Facts receives the barcode number and, like any website, your IP address. Product photos are loaded from its image servers. Your profile is not sent: the safety check and score are calculated on your device.
  • Label photos (optional). If you add your own Google Gemini API key and photograph a label, that one photo and your key are sent directly from your device to Google's Gemini API to read the text. Google handles it under its own terms and privacy policy for the key you created. Nothing is sent to Google unless you use one of these optional AI extras.
  • AI ingredient explanations (optional). If you add your key and tap “Explain these ingredients with AI”, the product’s name, ingredient list and nutrition figures are sent with your key to Google’s Gemini API. Your profile, allergies and history are not included.
  • Loading the app. The service that hosts the app's files may keep standard web server logs (such as IP address and time), as any website host does.

Camera

The camera is used for two things: reading barcodes and taking a label photo. Barcode reading happens entirely on your device; the video is not recorded, stored or uploaded. A label photo is sent to Google only as described above and is not kept by the app.

What we do not do

  • No accounts or sign-in.
  • No advertising, analytics, tracking or cookies.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data — we do not receive any.

Deleting your data

Open Settings → Delete all my data to erase your profile, history, counters and key from the device. Uninstalling the app or clearing its storage does the same.

Children

Greenlit is not directed at children under 13.

Not medical advice

Greenlit is an information tool, not a medical device, and does not provide medical, dietary or religious advice. Product data comes from Open Food Facts, a database written by volunteers, and from automated reading of label photos; optional AI explanations are written by Google’s AI. All of these can be incomplete, out of date or wrong, and recipes change.

Always read the physical label before eating a product, especially if you have an allergy or a medical condition. A “Safe for you” result means no problem was found in the available data; it is not a guarantee. If in doubt, ask the manufacturer or a qualified professional. Halal and kosher status generally cannot be proven from an ingredient list; the app only reports certification labels present in the data.
